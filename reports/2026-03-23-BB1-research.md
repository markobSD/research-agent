## Company Overview

BlinkLab Limited is an Australia-based digital healthcare company focused on AI-powered diagnostics for neurodevelopmental conditions. Founded as a Princeton University startup in 2021 and listed on the ASX in April 2024, BlinkLab has developed a smartphone-based diagnostic platform targeting autism, ADHD, schizophrenia, and other neurodevelopmental conditions.

The company's lead product, **BlinkLab Dx 1**, is an autism diagnostic adjunct that uses smartphone sensors, AI, and machine learning to deliver objective, reflex-based screening for children as young as 18 months. A second product, **BlinkLab Dx N**, targets ADHD diagnostics.

BlinkLab is headquartered in Perth, Australia.

## Recent ASX Announcements

### Trading Halt — National Autism Screening Initiative (approx. 19 March 2026)
BlinkLab requested a trading halt on its ASX securities ahead of the announcement of a national-level autism screening program. Trading was expected to resume no later than the start of normal trading on 23 March 2026. The company indicated the forthcoming announcement is material.

### Pivotal FDA Study — Clinical Site Network Completed (16 March 2026)
BlinkLab completed the clinical site network for its pivotal U.S. FDA 510(k) study of BlinkLab Dx 1. The University of Arkansas joined as the tenth and final participating site. The FDA-endorsed pivotal trial is designed to validate both usability and diagnostic accuracy, enrolling a minimum of 528 children aged 2–11 across leading U.S. autism clinical and academic institutions.

### FDA Pre-Submission Meeting (22 October 2025)
In a formal meeting with the U.S. FDA, the agency agreed with BlinkLab's pivotal 510(k) study design and confirmed a minimum performance threshold of >65% sensitivity and >65% specificity for regulatory clearance.

### Pilot Study Results (2025)
BlinkLab Dx 1 achieved 83.7% sensitivity and 84.7% specificity relative to clinical reference diagnosis in its pilot study — well above the FDA's minimum threshold.

## Sector Context

BlinkLab operates in the **AI-powered digital health diagnostics** sector, specifically targeting neurodevelopmental condition screening. Key market dynamics:

- The global AI healthcare market is projected to grow from US$11B (2021) to US$187B (2030).
- The autism diagnostics market alone is projected to reach US$5.41B by 2027.
- Traditional autism diagnosis typically occurs around age 5–6, with many specialty centers reporting wait times exceeding 4 months (some over a year). AI-powered smartphone screening addresses accessibility and early detection gaps.
- The FDA pathway (510(k) for diagnostic devices) is the critical regulatory milestone for U.S. market entry.

## Key Competitors

### 1. Cognoa — Canvas Dx (USA, acquired by Pearson)
- **Product:** Canvas Dx — first and only FDA-authorized digital autism diagnostic (ages 18 months–6 years).
- **Technology:** Machine learning + parent/caregiver questionnaires + uploaded video analysis.
- **Pricing:** ~US$2,280 per evaluation.
- **Status:** FDA authorized; commercially available.
- **Also developing:** ADHD, anxiety, and pediatric insomnia diagnostics.

### 2. EarliTec Diagnostics — EarliPoint Evaluation (USA)
- **Product:** EarliPoint Evaluation — FDA-cleared digital autism diagnostic (ages 16 months–2.5 years).
- **Technology:** Eye-tracking technology monitoring child focus during social interaction videos.
- **Status:** FDA 510(k) cleared (using Cognoa as predicate).

### 3. Duke University / SenseToKnow App (USA, Research Stage)
- **Product:** SenseToKnow — smartphone app for autism screening in toddlers (17 months–3 years).
- **Results:** 87.8% sensitivity, 80.8% specificity in an NIH-backed study (published in Nature Medicine).
- **Status:** Research stage; not yet commercially available.

### 4. LinusBio (USA)
- Developing autism diagnosis using hair strand biomarker analysis. Raised US$16M. Different technological approach.

**BlinkLab's differentiation:** Unlike competitors that rely on behavioral observation (questionnaires, video analysis, eye-tracking), BlinkLab uses objective biometric/reflex-based measures via smartphone sensors, which the company claims reduces reliance on subjective observation-based assessment.
